# The task-8 poem draw

Run 2026-11-06 under the selection rule in `task-8-segmentation-rule.md`, before
any prose was downloaded or matched. The draw is in `task-8-poem-draw.csv` next
to this file, and is reproduced exactly by

    uv run python src/draw_task8_poems.py

Two runs gave byte-identical output.

## What the record holds

`task-8-poem-draw.csv` has one row per poem, for every poem that matters to the
draw:

- `drawn`: one of the first 10 in the poet's seeded order, and kept (113).
- `excluded`: drawn, then judged unusable on inspection (7). The reason is in
  `excluded_because`.
- `replacement`: taken from beyond rank 10 to fill an exclusion's place (7).
- `reserve`: the next 5 in each poet's order. Any further replacement comes
  from here, in order, so it is fixed now rather than chosen later.

The final selection is the 120 rows marked `drawn` or `replacement`: 10 per
poet. Each poet's full seeded order is in `data/processed/task8_poem_draw.parquet`
(gitignored, regenerated by the script).

**Why translations are out:** in a translation the words are the poet's, but
the images and connections belong to the source poem. The test is about novel
expression *by this poet*.

**Fragments were kept:**

- Shelley, "Fragment: To the Moon";
- Burns, "My Girl she's Airy: A Fragment";
- Coleridge, "When Hope but made Tranquillity be felt (fragment)".

They are the poet's own words, and length is already governed by the 4-line
minimum.

**How the problems were found.**

- **Automated flags** (`flag` column) mark titles that suggest a translation or
  a fragment, exact duplicate texts, and poems whose first line occurs elsewhere
  in PoetryDB. The exact-text check missed the Keats pair because the copies
  differ by a heading; the first-line check catches that kind of duplicate.
- **Misattribution** cannot be detected automatically. The Hemans poem was
  found by reading the drawn poems, so a drawn list should always be read, not
  only filtered.
- **Two flags remain on the selection, both accepted.** Coleridge's "Lime-tree
  Bower" and Keats's "Ode To Autumn" each have a second copy elsewhere in
  PoetryDB; neither second copy is selected.

## What the selection looks like

- **Point counts:** a median of 25 distinct lines per poem.
- **Line length:** *k*, the mean words per line, runs from 4 to 18, the 18
  being Swinburne's long-lined self-parody "Nephelidia".
- **Form**, as a covariate only: irregular 48, single_block 23, ballad_stanza
  17, sonnet 13, regular_stanzaic 11, refrain_song 8.
- **Comparison B is thin.** Only **55 of 120** poems have at least 8 sentences:
  Robert Browning 9, Swinburne 8, Whitman 7, Coleridge 6, Shelley 5, Barrett
  Browning 4, Byron 4, Poe 3, Burns 3, Keats 3, Edward Thomas 2, Stevenson 1.
  B can be read only pooled across poets, never per poet. Comparison A is
  unaffected.

## Carried forward to the cutting stage

- **Numeral-only lines are dropped before n and k are computed.** Twelve
  selected poems contain lines that are nothing but a section numeral. Barrett
  Browning's "Mother and Poet" has 20 ("I" to "XX") and Whitman's "Proud Music
  of the Storm" 15. Each would otherwise become a meaningless point in the
  cloud. The draw is unaffected: every selected poem stays within 4–200 distinct
  lines after stripping. So *n* and *k* in the record are the draw-time values,
  and they are recomputed after stripping when the prose is cut.
- **Burns is also reported on his own**, alongside Edward Thomas. His poems
  are largely in Scots and his letters in standard English. A model trained
  mostly on modern English may place dialect words idiosyncratically, which
  would spread his poems' points wider. That is the same direction as the
  claim, so his contribution must be visible separately, as well as through the
  leave-one-poet-out check.
